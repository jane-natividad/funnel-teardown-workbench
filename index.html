Funnel, torn down: an outside-in teardown where every finding says how you know it

Jane Natividad  ·  jane-natividad.github.io  ·  View on GitHub

Most funnel teardowns blur three very different things: what you saw yourself, what a customer review or listing says, and what you're guessing. This workbench forces the distinction. Every finding is tagged by stage, severity, and evidence type, so the prioritized summary at the end can say — honestly — which problems are confirmed and which are hypotheses to validate on day one. Two fictional products show the two funnel shapes that matter most in digital health: direct-to-consumer and clinician-gated.

        What this method learned the hard way
        
  
        
    
        1
        Check the claim against the live sourceA trust badge on the homepage and the live review score it links to can disagree. Usually it's a stale widget, not deception — but it's checkable, so check it.
        
    
        2
        Find out who actually convertsIn a clinician-gated product the patient can't sign up at all. The conversion that drives growth is a clinician finishing training and staying active — a completely different funnel to optimize.
        
    
        3
        Read the renewal, not just the first orderFirst-order offers are explicit; what happens on month two often isn't. That's where reviews get angry.
        
    
        4
        Stop before you become a real customerWalk the flow up to name, email, and payment, and say so. The scope box is part of the output, not a footnote.
